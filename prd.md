Hostel Fix

The problem

Every hostel has a logbook at the porter's lodge, and every student knows how it goes. Your door lock breaks on Monday, so you write it down. On Wednesday you walk back to ask if anyone has seen it, and the porter says they'll "look into it." By Friday you're still sleeping with a chair wedged under the handle, your laptop is hidden in your bag, and you've started wondering if the page has been torn out.

Nothing about that is dramatic. A lock, a pipe, a socket, a bed frame: the repair itself is usually simple. What wears students down is the silence that follows the report. You don't know if anyone saw it, if it's been assigned, or if it will ever be fixed.

Who feels this

* Students live with the discomfort every day, and sometimes with real danger, like exposed wires or doors that don't lock properly. Many end up paying out of their own pockets to repair school property, even though they've already paid their accommodation fees.
* Porters and wardens are not the villains here. They're buried in repeated verbal complaints, often about the same problem, and they have no simple way to see which repairs are done and which are still hanging.
* Technicians are handed vague job descriptions like "problem in Room 12," with no photos and no detail. They arrive without the right tools or replacement parts and have to come back another day.

Why today's workarounds don't work

* The logbook: Pages get ignored or torn out, nothing is timestamped, and students have to keep walking back just to ask for an update.
* WhatsApp and word of mouth: Reports get swallowed by busy group chats, often with no room number and no photo. Weeks later, nobody can look back and prove what was reported or when.
* Paying out of pocket: It's unfair to students who have already paid for their accommodation, and an unapproved, amateur fix can easily make the damage worse.

None of these are bad-faith failures. They're what happens when there's no shared, visible system that everyone can see and trust.

The idea

Hostel Fix is a simple, mobile-friendly app built around one promise: when you report a problem, you'll always know what's happening with it.

A student reports the issue with a photo. The hall admin reviews it and assigns it to a technician. The technician does the work and uploads proof. And the student, the person who actually lives with the problem, confirms whether it's really fixed. We haven't chosen a tech stack yet, and that's deliberate. For now, the focus is on getting the workflow right, because that's where the real problem lives.

What we're building first

Who uses it

1. Students report issues, follow their progress, and confirm repairs.
2. Hostel admins (porters and wardens) review reports, set priority, and assign technicians.
3. Technicians see their assigned jobs, update progress, and upload proof of the repair.

What it does

1. Quick reporting. Nobody wants to fill out a long form about a leaking tap. Hostel, block, and room number are filled in automatically from the student's profile, and for shared spaces like bathrooms and corridors they can simply choose "Common Area." From there, it takes under a minute: pick a category (Electrical, Plumbing, Carpentry/Locks, or Other), mark it Normal or Emergency, add a short note, and attach a photo.

2. "Me too" instead of duplicates. If one person has already reported the broken corridor light or the leaking shared pipe, everyone else on that floor sees the open ticket and can tap "Confirm Issue (+1)" instead of filing the same complaint again. Admins aren't flooded with repeats, and a growing count of confirmations tells them how many people the problem affects.

3. A clear five-step tracker. Every report moves through the same stages, so no one has to guess:

1. Submitted: the student logs the issue.
2. Assigned: the admin gives it to a technician.
3. In Progress: the technician is working on it.
4. Fixed (Pending Approval): the technician marks it done and uploads an "after" photo.
5. Closed: the student taps "Confirm Fixed," or "Reopen" if the problem is still there. If the student doesn't respond, the ticket closes on its own after 72 hours.

That last step matters. The person who lives with the problem gets the final say on whether it's solved, not just the person who did the repair.

4. A simple admin dashboard. Wardens get one clear list of open tickets, filterable by urgency and category. Any emergency that's been sitting open for more than 24 hours is flagged, so the things that can't wait don't quietly slip through the cracks.

How we'll know it's working

* Faster repairs: fewer days between a report being submitted and the fix being confirmed.
* Fixed the first time: a higher share of repairs that students don't have to reopen, which means the job was done properly the first time.
* People actually use it: more complaints going through Hostel Fix than through the paper logbook. If students trust it, they'll use it, and that is the real test.

What could go wrong, and what we'll do about it

* Weak hostel Wi-Fi: Photos are compressed automatically so they upload on slow connections, and students can save drafts offline that send as soon as they're back online.
* Fake or prank reports: Sign-up is limited to verified matric numbers and official room allocation lists, so every report comes from a real resident.
* Staff pushback: Porters can log tickets on behalf of walk-in students who don't use smartphones. The goal is to make their job easier, not to replace them, and nobody should be left out.

Conclusion

Hostel Fix is about more than fixing sockets and locks. It's about making sure students are heard, staff can see what needs doing, and technicians know what to expect. Report, assign, fix, confirm: a small first step that means no student has to wonder whether anyone noticed.
